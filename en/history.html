The history of Komodo Dragon (Varanus Komodoensis)

Origins

The Komodo dragon (Vanarus Komodoensis) is the largest lizard species in the world and originates from eastern Indonesia, particulary the islands of Komodo, Rinca, Flores, Gili, Motang, and Gili Dasami, Scientifically, the Komodo dragon belongs to the Varanidae family, which is a group of ancient monitor lizards that have existed for millions of years. Scientist believe that Komodo dragons migrated from Australia to the Indonesian archipelago via lands during the Ice Age. As climate changes occorred and sea levels rose, Komodo dtagons became isolated on several idlands and become the apex predator in their habitats.

Discovery

The Indonesian people have known about the existence of the Komodo dragon for a long time, and local residents often refer to it as “ora.” In 1910, a Dutch officer named Lieutenant J.K.H. van Steyn van Hensbroek heard reports of a giant “land crocodile” on Komodo Island. He then sent reports and photographs to the Dutch colonial government, and this information attracted the attention of European scientists.

Conservation Efforts

As human settlements expanded and climate change occurred, the Komodo dragon population began to face threats from poaching, a decline in prey, and habitat destruction. To protect the Komodo dragon, the Indonesian government established Komodo National Park in 1980, which was later designated as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1991. This area aims to protect the Komodo dragon and its ecosystem.
